# MGNREGA — General FAQ

### What is MGNREGA?
MGNREGA (Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Act) is an Indian labour law and social security measure. It is the world's largest rights-based employment guarantee programme, legally entitling every rural household to at least 100 days of guaranteed wage employment in a financial year, provided its adult members volunteer to do unskilled manual work.

### What does MGNREGA stand for?
Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Act.

### What is the difference between NREGA and MGNREGA?
NREGA (National Rural Employment Guarantee Act) was the scheme's original 2005 name. It was renamed MGNREGA in 2009 in honour of Mahatma Gandhi. Both names refer to the same scheme.

### When was MGNREGA enacted and implemented?
The Act was passed by Parliament in 2005 and came into force on 2 February 2006, initially in 200 of the most backward districts, before being extended in phases to cover the whole country.

### What is the main objective of MGNREGA?
Its primary objective is rural poverty alleviation and livelihood security — guaranteeing wage employment on demand to rural households, thereby strengthening the "right to work." Secondary objectives include creating durable rural assets (roads, ponds, irrigation works), strengthening natural resource management, empowering women and marginalised groups, and deepening local democracy through Gram Panchayats.

### What are the key benefits of MGNREGA?
- Legal guarantee of up to 100 days of wage employment per household per year.
- A demand-driven safety net: work must be provided within 15 days of a request, failing which an unemployment allowance is payable.
- Equal wages for men and women, helping close the rural gender wage gap.
- Creation of durable community assets like water conservation structures, rural roads, and land development works.
- Financial inclusion, since wages are paid directly into workers' bank or post office accounts.
- Decentralised planning and implementation through Gram Panchayats and Gram Sabhas.

### Who is eligible to work under MGNREGA?
Any adult member (18 years or older) of a rural household is eligible to register and demand work, regardless of income level — there is no poverty-line restriction. The household must apply for and receive a job card from the local Gram Panchayat.

### Is there an income or poverty criterion to apply for MGNREGA work?
No. Unlike many welfare schemes, MGNREGA has no income or poverty-line eligibility test — any rural household willing to do unskilled manual work can apply, regardless of economic status.

### What is a job card?
A job card is an official document issued free of cost by the Gram Panchayat to a registered rural household. It records the identity of adult members willing to work and their history of employment demanded and provided under the scheme. It must be produced when applying for work.

### How do I apply for a job card?
An adult member of a rural household submits an application (a simple form with a photograph) to the Gram Panchayat. The Panchayat conducts a local verification and is required to issue the job card within 15 days, free of cost.

### How does a household get work under MGNREGA?
An adult member of a registered household submits a written (or oral) application for work to the Gram Panchayat or Programme Officer. Employment must be provided within 15 days of the application; if it isn't, the applicant becomes entitled to a daily unemployment allowance.

### What is the unemployment allowance?
If the local implementing agency fails to provide work within 15 days of a valid application, the state government must pay an unemployment allowance to the applicant, at a rate not less than one-fourth of the wage rate for the first 30 days and not less than one-half thereafter, for the period of delay.

### How many days of employment are guaranteed under MGNREGA?
Up to 100 days of guaranteed wage employment per household per financial year. Some states supplement this — for example, additional days have been provided in drought-affected or disaster-hit areas, and enhanced entitlements exist for households with a member with a disability in certain states.

### How is the MGNREGA wage rate decided, and does it vary by state?
Wages are notified separately for each state/UT by the Ministry of Rural Development, based on the Consumer Price Index for Agricultural Labourers (CPI-AL), and are revised annually (effective 1 April). Rates vary significantly by state, since cost of living and agricultural wage benchmarks differ. Because rates change every year, the exact current figure for a specific state should be checked against the latest official notification rather than assumed.

### What kind of work is done under MGNREGA?
Permissible works are mostly labour-intensive, unskilled manual work focused on strengthening the rural livelihood resource base, such as: water conservation and harvesting structures, drought-proofing (afforestation, tree plantation), irrigation canals, renovation of traditional water bodies, land development, flood control, rural connectivity (all-weather roads), and works on land owned by SC/ST, small/marginal farmers, or beneficiaries of land reform.

### Who implements MGNREGA?
It is implemented by the Ministry of Rural Development (MoRD) at the central level, in partnership with state governments. Gram Panchayats play the lead role in planning, approving, and executing works, with Gram Sabhas recommending which works to prioritise.

### How is MGNREGA funded (Centre-State cost sharing)?
The central government bears 100% of the cost of unskilled labour wages and 75% of the cost of material and skilled/semi-skilled wages for permissible works; state governments bear the remaining material cost, the unemployment allowance (if payable), and administrative expenses of the State Employment Guarantee Council.

### What is the role of the Gram Sabha in MGNREGA?
The Gram Sabha (village assembly) recommends the works to be taken up in its area, conducts social audits of all projects executed within the Gram Panchayat, and is central to the scheme's participatory, bottom-up planning process.

### What is a social audit under MGNREGA?
A social audit is a public, participatory review — conducted by the Gram Sabha at least once every six months — of MGNREGA works, expenditure, and muster rolls in the local area, meant to ensure transparency and accountability and to check for irregularities such as fake job cards or inflated measurements.

### How and when are MGNREGA wages paid?
Wages must be paid within 15 days of the date on which work was done (i.e., of muster roll closure), directly into the worker's bank or post office account via electronic Fund Management System / Direct Benefit Transfer. Delay beyond this period entitles the worker to compensation for delayed payment.

### What happens if MGNREGA authorities delay wage payment?
Workers are entitled to compensation for delayed wage payment, calculated as a percentage of the unpaid wage for each day of delay beyond the 15-day payment window, funded by the central government.

### Does MGNREGA provide equal wages to men and women?
Yes. The Act mandates equal wages for men and women for the same work, and at least one-third of the beneficiaries must be women.

### Are there special provisions for persons with disabilities or vulnerable groups?
Yes. Provisions include facilitation for persons with disabilities to take up work suited to their ability (or receive a wage-equivalent stipend where fully unable to work), priority for SC/ST households and other vulnerable groups, and worksite facilities such as drinking water, shade, first aid, and crèche facilities for children under six where five or more children are present.

### What worksite facilities is a MGNREGA worker legally entitled to?
Drinking water, shade for rest, a first-aid kit, and crèche/childcare arrangements at worksites with children, are mandated by the Act's Schedule II.

### Can I choose what type of work I do under MGNREGA?
Work is assigned from the list of approved, permissible unskilled manual works planned for the area (as recommended by the Gram Sabha and approved by the Gram Panchayat); workers don't individually select the exact task, but the scheme aims to provide work within or near the applicant's village.

### What is the distance limit for providing MGNREGA work?
As far as possible, work should be provided within 5 km of the applicant's residence; if it is provided beyond that, workers are entitled to extra wages (travel/subsistence allowance) to compensate for the additional distance.

### How does someone register a complaint or grievance under MGNREGA?
Grievances can be raised with the Gram Panchayat, Programme Officer, or District Programme Coordinator, and escalated to the state's MGNREGA grievance redressal mechanism or Ombudsperson where constituted. Many states also run toll-free helplines and online grievance portals linked to the national MGNREGA MIS.

### Who is a MGNREGA Ombudsperson?
An independent grievance-redressal authority appointed at the district level in many states to receive, investigate, and resolve complaints related to the implementation of the scheme, including delayed payments, denial of work, or corruption.

### Can MGNREGA work be denied to a household?
No — MGNREGA is a demand-driven, legally guaranteed scheme. A willing and eligible applicant cannot be denied work; failure to provide it within 15 days triggers the unemployment allowance instead.

### What documents are needed to apply for MGNREGA?
Typically a completed application form, a passport-size photograph, and proof of residence in the Gram Panchayat area (verified locally); no income certificate or poverty documentation is required.

### Where can official, up-to-date MGNREGA information be checked?
The Ministry of Rural Development's MGNREGA Management Information System (MIS) publishes state-, district-, and panchayat-wise data on employment generated, expenditure, works, and worker demographics, and is the authoritative source for current figures, notifications, and wage rates.
