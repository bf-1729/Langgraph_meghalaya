# MGNREGA (Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Act) — Complete Scheme Reference

## 1. Overview

The Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Act (MGNREGA) is an Indian labour law and social security measure. It is described as the world's largest rights-based employment guarantee programme. It legally entitles every rural household in India to at least 100 days of guaranteed wage employment in a financial year, provided that adult members of the household volunteer to do unskilled manual work.

The scheme operates on a demand-driven basis: a household applies for work, and the government is legally obliged to provide it within a fixed time or pay an unemployment allowance instead. This makes MGNREGA fundamentally different from most welfare or subsidy schemes, because employment under it is a **legal right**, not a discretionary benefit.

The scheme's implementing ministry at the central level is the **Ministry of Rural Development (MoRD), Government of India**. The scheme is officially referred to as MGNREGS (Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Scheme) — the "Act" (MGNREGA) is the law, and the "Scheme" (MGNREGS) is the programme run under that law; in everyday use, both names are used interchangeably.

## 2. Naming History: NREGA vs MGNREGA

- **2005:** The scheme was originally legislated as the **National Rural Employment Guarantee Act (NREGA)**.
- **2 October 2009:** NREGA was renamed the **Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Act (MGNREGA)**, in honour of Mahatma Gandhi.
- Both names — NREGA and MGNREGA — refer to the same law and the same scheme; MGNREGA is the current official name.
- The scheme was first proposed in concept form as early as 1991, by then Prime Minister P.V. Narasimha Rao, though it was not enacted at that time.

## 3. Legislative Timeline and Launch

- **23 August 2005 (also cited as passed/enacted around this date):** The National Rural Employment Guarantee Act was passed by the Parliament of India, during the tenure of Prime Minister Dr. Manmohan Singh (2004–2014).
- **2 February 2006:** The Act came into force and the scheme was formally launched. The launch is most widely associated with **Anantapur district, Andhra Pradesh** (some accounts specifically cite the village of Bandlapalli in Anantapur district), where Prime Minister Manmohan Singh personally distributed the first job cards. The scheme was simultaneously rolled out across **200 of the most backward districts** of the country in this first phase (some sources also note parallel inaugural events in other states, such as Haryana, on the same date, since the rollout covered many districts nationwide on day one).
- **National Advisory Council (NAC),** chaired by Sonia Gandhi at the time, played a key advisory role in shaping the Act around the principle of the "Right to Work."
- **2007–08 (Phase II):** The scheme was extended to a further **130 districts**.
- **1 April 2008 (Phase III):** MGNREGA was extended to cover the **entire rural area of the country**, spanning all States and Union Territories — at that point covering roughly 614–626 districts, over 6,000 blocks, and around 2.65 lakh Gram Panchayats.
- By **2013–14**, the scheme's reported coverage had grown to roughly 644 districts, 6,576 blocks, and 7.78 lakh villages.
- Today, MGNREGA effectively covers all rural districts of India (with limited exceptions, such as districts with 100% urban population).

## 4. Objectives of the Scheme

MGNREGA's objectives, as commonly described, include:

1. **Livelihood security / poverty alleviation:** Enhancing livelihood security in rural areas by guaranteeing wage employment on demand, thereby providing an income safety net and reducing rural poverty.
2. **Legal guarantee of the "Right to Work":** Making the government legally accountable for providing rural employment on demand — a rights-based entitlement, not a discretionary scheme.
3. **Creation of durable rural infrastructure/assets:** Building productive community assets such as roads, irrigation canals, ponds, water conservation structures, and land development works that strengthen the rural livelihood resource base.
4. **Strengthening natural resource management:** Encouraging works related to water conservation, drought-proofing, afforestation, and soil and land productivity.
5. **Reducing distress migration:** By guaranteeing local employment, the scheme aims to reduce forced seasonal migration of rural workers to cities in search of work.
6. **Empowering women:** Mandating that women make up at least one-third of the workforce and receive equal wages to men, and encouraging direct wage payment to women's own bank accounts.
7. **Deepening local democracy / decentralisation:** Strengthening Panchayati Raj Institutions (Gram Panchayats and Gram Sabhas) by giving them a central role in planning, approving, and executing works.
8. **Promoting social equity:** Prioritising SC/ST households, small and marginal farmers, and other disadvantaged groups, and fostering inclusion through equal wages and non-discriminatory access to work.

## 5. Key Legal/Structural Features

- **Legal entitlement, not a scheme benefit:** Employment under MGNREGA is a statutory right. If work is not provided within the stipulated time, the applicant is entitled to compensation (unemployment allowance) by law.
- **Demand-driven, not target-driven:** Unlike supply-driven schemes with fixed beneficiary quotas, MGNREGA work is generated in response to actual demand from households.
- **100 days of guaranteed employment per household per financial year** — the guarantee is per household (not per individual), split among adult members as the household decides. Some states provide additional days (e.g., for drought-hit areas, or enhanced entitlement of up to 150 days for households with a member with a disability in select cases).
- **Universal rural eligibility, no poverty-line test:** Any rural household is eligible to apply, regardless of income; there is no BPL (Below Poverty Line) restriction.
- **Equal wages for men and women** for the same work.
- **At least one-third of beneficiaries must be women.**
- **Decentralised implementation** through the three-tier Panchayati Raj system, with Gram Panchayats as the primary implementing agency at the village level.

## 6. Eligibility Criteria

To be eligible for work under MGNREGA, an applicant must:
- Be an **adult** (18 years of age or older).
- Be a **member of a rural household** (i.e., ordinarily resident in a rural area covered by the scheme).
- Be **willing to do unskilled manual work**.
- Apply for **registration** with the local Gram Panchayat to obtain a **job card**.

There is **no income, caste, or poverty-line eligibility criterion** — eligibility is based purely on rural residence, adulthood, and willingness to do unskilled manual labour. Any number of adult members from the same household may register on a single job card and seek work.

## 7. Registration and the Job Card

- A **job card** is a document issued **free of cost** by the Gram Panchayat to a registered rural household.
- It records the names and photographs of all adult household members willing to work, and maintains a running record of employment demanded, employment provided, and wages paid over time.
- **Application:** An adult household member submits an application (a simple form, typically with a passport-size photograph) to the Gram Panchayat.
- **Verification:** The Gram Panchayat conducts a local verification of residence and household details.
- **Issuance:** The job card must be issued within **15 days** of application, free of charge.
- The job card must be produced/referenced whenever the household applies for work, and it is the household's primary proof of registration under the scheme.

## 8. Applying for Work (Demand for Employment)

1. A registered adult household member submits a **written or oral application for work** to the Gram Panchayat or the Programme Officer, indicating the period and number of days of work required.
2. A **dated receipt** for the application should be issued to the applicant, since this date starts the clock on the government's 15-day obligation.
3. The Gram Panchayat/Programme Officer must **allocate work within 15 days** of the date of application.
4. Work should, as far as possible, be provided **within 5 km of the applicant's residence**; if provided beyond this distance, workers are entitled to additional wages (a travel/subsistence allowance) to compensate for the extra distance.
5. If work **cannot be provided within 15 days**, the applicant becomes entitled to a **daily unemployment allowance** instead (see Section 10).

## 9. Planning and Selection of Works

- The **Gram Sabha** (village assembly) plays the lead role in **recommending which works** should be taken up in its area, based on local needs.
- The **Gram Panchayat** prepares a **shelf of works/projects** in advance (an annual labour budget/action plan), technically and administratively approved, so that work is ready to be allocated as soon as demand arises.
- **Permissible categories of work** under MGNREGA (Schedule I of the Act) mainly focus on strengthening the rural livelihood resource base and include:
  - Water conservation and water harvesting structures (check dams, farm ponds, percolation tanks).
  - Drought-proofing, including afforestation and tree plantation.
  - Irrigation canals, including micro- and minor irrigation works.
  - Renovation of traditional water bodies, including de-silting of tanks.
  - Land development works, particularly on land owned by SC/ST households, small and marginal farmers, and beneficiaries of land reforms or the Indira Awaas Yojana/PMAY.
  - Flood control and protection works, including drainage in waterlogged areas.
  - Rural connectivity — construction of all-weather roads to provide access.
  - Any other work notified by the central government in consultation with state governments (which over time has expanded to include works like rural sanitation infrastructure, Anganwadi construction, and playgrounds, subject to guidelines).

## 10. Unemployment Allowance

- If the implementing agency fails to provide employment within **15 days** of a valid application, the applicant becomes entitled to a daily **unemployment allowance**, paid by the **state government**.
- The rate is **not less than one-fourth of the wage rate** for the first 30 days of a financial year for which the allowance is payable, and **not less than one-half of the wage rate** for any subsequent days in that year.
- This provision is what gives the "guarantee" in the scheme's name real legal force — it converts the employment promise into an enforceable financial obligation on the state if work is not provided.

## 11. Wage Rates and Wage Payment

- **Wage determination:** Wage rates are notified separately for each state/Union Territory by the Ministry of Rural Development, using the **Consumer Price Index for Agricultural Labourers (CPI-AL)** as the basis, and are revised **annually, typically effective from 1 April** of each financial year.
- **State variation:** Because CPI-AL and cost-of-living benchmarks differ across states, MGNREGA wage rates vary considerably from one state to another. Wage rates should always be checked against the current official notification, since they change every year.
- **Equal wages:** Men and women performing the same work receive the same wage rate.
- **Piece-rate/task-based system:** Wages are generally calculated based on the quantity/quality of work completed against a pre-defined **Schedule of Rates (SoR)**, measured by technical staff, rather than a flat daily attendance wage — in effect a task-based (piece-rate) system, subject to a minimum standard.
- **Payment timeline:** Wages must be paid within **15 days of the date on which the work was done** — i.e., within 15 days of muster roll closure.
- **Payment mode:** Wages are paid **directly into the worker's bank or post office account**, via the electronic Fund Management System (eFMS) / Direct Benefit Transfer (DBT), rather than in cash, to ensure transparency and reduce leakages.
- **Delay compensation:** If wage payment is delayed beyond the 15-day window, the worker is entitled to **compensation for delayed payment**, calculated as a percentage of the unpaid wage for each day of delay, funded by the central government.

## 12. Muster Rolls, Measurement, and Worksite Management

- **Muster rolls** are the official attendance and work records maintained at each worksite, listing workers present and days/output recorded — these form the basis for wage calculation and are also central to social audits.
- **Measurement of work:** Technical staff measure completed work against the Schedule of Rates to determine wages payable.
- **Worksite facilities** mandated under Schedule II of the Act include:
  - Drinking water at the worksite.
  - Shade for periods of rest.
  - A first-aid kit for emergency treatment of minor injuries.
  - Crèche/childcare facilities where five or more children under the age of six are present at a worksite, so that women workers are not held back by childcare responsibilities.

### Worker Safety and Accident Compensation
- Workers injured in the course of MGNREGA employment are entitled to **free medical treatment**, including hospitalisation costs where required.
- A worker unable to work due to a worksite injury is entitled to a **daily allowance** for the period of incapacity.
- In case of **death or permanent disability** resulting from a worksite accident, the family/worker is entitled to an **ex-gratia payment**.
- If a worksite injury or death occurs to a **child** accompanying a worker (e.g., at a crèche), the Act also provides for compensation in that case.

## 13. Institutional and Funding Structure

**Implementing bodies and governance hierarchy:**
- **Central Employment Guarantee Council (CEGC):** Constituted under **Section 10** of the Act, chaired by the Union Minister of Rural Development. It advises the central government on all matters concerning implementation of the Act, reviews the monitoring and grievance-redressal mechanisms, recommends improvements, and prepares the scheme's **annual report to be laid before Parliament**.
- **Ministry of Rural Development (MoRD), Government of India:** The nodal ministry responsible for policy, wage notification, and release of central funds to states.
- **State Employment Guarantee Council (SEGC):** Advises the state government on implementation, evaluates and monitors the scheme, recommends works and improvements, and reports to the state government.
- **State Employment Guarantee Fund (SEGF):** A dedicated fund that every state government is required to establish to finance its share of scheme costs (unemployment allowance, state's share of material cost, and administrative expenses).
- **District Programme Coordinator (DPC):** Generally the district administration (often the District Collector/Magistrate), responsible for coordinating implementation across all blocks in the district, and for compiling and forwarding the district's Labour Budget to the state government.
- **Programme Officer (PO):** Positioned at the block level; chiefly responsible for ensuring that anyone applying for work is provided employment within 15 days, along with overall block-level implementation.
- **Gram Panchayat:** The primary implementing agency at the village level — responsible for registration, job card issuance, work allocation, muster roll maintenance, and execution of most works, guided by the Gram Sabha's recommendations.
- **Gram Rozgar Sahayak (Employment Guarantee Assistant):** A dedicated village-level functionary engaged to support the Gram Panchayat with day-to-day scheme administration — registration, applications, muster rolls, and record-keeping.
- **Village Employment Council / Mate (worksite supervisor):** In some states, a local body or an appointed worksite supervisor ("Mate") assists with on-ground planning of works and daily supervision of worksite attendance and progress.

**Cost-sharing pattern (Centre–State):**
- The **central government bears 100% of the cost of unskilled labour wages**.
- The central government bears **75% of the cost of material and the wages of skilled/semi-skilled workers** for permissible works.
- The **state government bears the remaining 25% of material costs**, along with the **full cost of the unemployment allowance** (if payable) and the administrative expenses of the State Employment Guarantee Council.
- (Some individual state-run variants of the scheme have used different centre-state ratios, such as 90:10, for their own supplementary components — but the base MGNREGA cost-sharing formula above is the one prescribed centrally.)

## 13A. Financial Management and Expenditure Norms

- **Wage-to-material expenditure ratio:** At the district level, at least **60% of total annual expenditure** must be spent on the wage component, with **no more than 40%** spent on material costs, for permissible works — a rule intended to keep the scheme's focus on labour-intensive employment generation rather than material-heavy construction.
- **Administrative expenditure cap:** A defined ceiling (a small single-digit percentage of total expenditure) is allowed for administrative costs such as staff salaries and office expenses, funded partly by the Centre and partly by the state.
- **Fund flow:** Central funds are released to states based on the agreed Labour Budget, the state's opening balance, any pending liabilities from the previous year, and overall performance; since MGNREGA is demand-driven, the Ministry seeks additional funds as and when actual demand for work exceeds the initial allocation.
- **Fund Transfer Order (FTO):** The administrative instruction generated after wage computation that triggers the actual crediting of wages into a worker's bank/post office account — tracking the FTO status is how officials and workers can check where a payment is in the disbursal pipeline.

## 13B. Digital Systems Used in Implementation

- **National Mobile Monitoring System (NMMS) App:** Used to capture worker attendance at worksites through **two time-stamped, geo-tagged photographs per day**, for all works other than Individual Beneficiary Works. It has been enhanced over time with features like eye-blink liveness detection, head-count verification, and Mate-ID mapping with muster rolls, to reduce fraudulent attendance entries.
- **Aadhaar-Based Payment System (ABPS):** The mandated mode for crediting wages to workers, using each worker's 12-digit Aadhaar number as their financial address. It requires the worker's job card and bank account to both be seeded with Aadhaar and mapped with the National Payments Corporation of India (NPCI) — if this mapping is missing or fails, wage credit can be delayed or misrouted.
- **MGNREGA Management Information System (MIS):** The central online portal (maintained by MoRD) where employment, expenditure, works, muster roll, and worker-category data are recorded and published at the state/district/panchayat level, supporting transparency and monitoring.
- **Geo-tagging of assets:** Completed and in-progress works are geo-tagged and photographed at various stages, allowing remote verification of physical progress against reported expenditure.

## 13C. Penalties and Non-Compliance Provisions

- **Section 25 (penalty for non-compliance):** Provides for a fine (historically up to ₹1,000) on any officer or implementing agency that, without reasonable cause, contravenes provisions of the Act — for example, failing to register applicants, provide employment, or make timely payments.
- **Section 27 (fund stoppage for non-compliance):** Empowers the central government to **withhold or stop the release of funds** to a state found non-compliant with central directives on implementation (this provision has been invoked in practice against specific states over compliance disputes).
- **Delay compensation:** Beyond penalties on officials, workers themselves are entitled to compensation for delayed wage payment, calculated as a percentage of the unpaid wage for each day of delay beyond the payment window — a distinct, worker-facing remedy separate from the penalty provisions above.
- **Misappropriation recovery:** Any amount found misappropriated under social audit or inspection is recoverable from the responsible official/agency under the state's prevailing revenue recovery laws.

## 13D. Annual Planning Cycle — the Labour Budget

- **Labour Budget (LB):** MGNREGA implementation is planned annually through a bottom-up "Labour Budget" exercise:
  1. **Gram Sabha** identifies, prioritises, and recommends permissible works for the coming year, based on local needs.
  2. The **Gram Panchayat** consolidates these recommendations and forwards them to the Programme Officer.
  3. Proposals are aggregated at the **block** and then **district** level.
  4. The **District Programme Coordinator (DPC)** is required to forward the approved district Labour Budget and Development Plan for the next financial year to the **state government by 31 December** each year.
  5. States compile and submit their consolidated Labour Budgets to the **central government (MoRD)**, which uses them, along with opening balances and prior-year liabilities, as the basis for annual central fund allocation.
- This planning cycle is what ensures a technically sanctioned "shelf of works" is ready in advance, so that work can be allocated quickly once a household applies (see Section 9).

## 13E. Convergence with Other Government Schemes

MGNREGA works and funds are frequently converged with other rural development programmes to maximise impact, including:
- **Housing schemes (e.g., Pradhan Mantri Awas Yojana-Gramin):** MGNREGA provides wage-employment support (unskilled labour days) for beneficiaries constructing their own PMAY-G houses, effectively supplementing the housing scheme's own assistance.
- **Watershed development and afforestation programmes:** Joint planning of water conservation, land development, and plantation works.
- **Swachh Bharat Mission (Gramin):** Convergence for the labour component of individual household toilet construction and related sanitation infrastructure.
- **Anganwadi and other rural infrastructure:** Construction of Anganwadi centres, playgrounds, and similar community infrastructure, where permitted under scheme guidelines.
- Such convergence allows a single physical work (e.g., a beneficiary's house or toilet) to draw on MGNREGA's wage component while another scheme funds the materials/technical cost, so long as both schemes' guidelines permit it.

## 14. Special Provisions for Vulnerable Groups

- **Persons with disabilities:** The scheme facilitates work suited to an individual's ability; where a person is fully unable to perform manual work, some states provide a wage-equivalent stipend or alternate support.
- **SC/ST households and other vulnerable groups:** Given priority in planning of works, particularly land-development works benefiting their own land.
- **Women:** Guaranteed at least one-third of total employment opportunities, equal wages, worksite childcare facilities, and preference for wage payment into their own individual bank accounts to promote financial inclusion and independence.
- **Drought/disaster-hit areas:** Some states have provided enhanced day entitlements (beyond the standard 100 days) during drought years or natural disasters, subject to state government notification.
- **Scheduled Tribe households in forest areas:** Households holding land rights under the Forest Rights Act, 2006, with no other private property, are eligible for an enhanced entitlement of up to **150 days** of employment in a financial year, instead of the standard 100 days.

## 15. Social Audit and Transparency Mechanisms

- **Social audit:** A mandatory, participatory public review of MGNREGA works, expenditure, and muster rolls, to be conducted by the **Gram Sabha at least once every six months**.
- **Purpose:** To publicly verify that works were actually carried out, that wages match muster roll records, and to detect irregularities such as fake job cards, ghost workers, or inflated work measurements.
- **MGNREGA MIS (Management Information System):** A centralised online portal maintained by the Ministry of Rural Development, publishing state-, district-, and panchayat-wise data on employment generated, works completed, expenditure, and worker demographics, updated regularly, and used to promote transparency and enable public scrutiny.
- **Geo-tagging and digital monitoring:** Many works are geo-tagged and photographed at different stages of completion, and payments are increasingly linked to the National Electronic Fund Management System (NeFMS) to reduce delays and leakages.
- **National Level Monitors (NLMs):** Independent monitors deputed periodically by the central government to visit states/districts, inspect ongoing works and records on the ground, and report directly to the Ministry of Rural Development on implementation quality and irregularities — a central-level oversight layer distinct from the state's own SEGC monitoring.
- **Proactive disclosure requirements:** Implementing agencies are required to display scheme information locally through means such as wall writings at worksites and citizen/information boards at the Gram Panchayat, listing sanctioned works, expenditure, and worker entitlements, so that information is available to the community without a formal request.
- **Social audit vs. grievance/RTI channels:** A social audit is a scheduled, community-led review of an area's overall MGNREGA implementation, distinct from an individual **grievance complaint** (raised by an affected person) or a **Right to Information (RTI)** request (used to obtain specific official records); all three are available and complementary transparency tools under the scheme.

## 15A. Categorisation of Workers and Wages

- Work under MGNREGA is generally **unskilled manual labour**, but implementation also recognises **semi-skilled** and **skilled** labour inputs for certain permissible works (e.g., masonry for structures), which are costed and paid differently from the unskilled wage rate.
- **Unskilled wages** are entirely centrally funded and calculated on a task/piece-rate basis against the Schedule of Rates.
- **Semi-skilled/skilled wages** fall under the "material and skilled/semi-skilled labour" cost head, of which the Centre bears 75% and the state 25% (see Section 13).
- MGNREGA MIS reporting also captures worker data by **social category** (SC, ST, and others) to track equitable access and inclusion across social groups, alongside gender-wise participation data.

## 16. Grievance Redressal

- Complaints (e.g., denial of work, delayed wages, irregularities in muster rolls, corruption) can be raised at multiple levels:
  - **Gram Panchayat / Programme Officer** (first point of contact).
  - **District Programme Coordinator.**
  - **State-level grievance redressal mechanisms**, including toll-free helplines and online grievance portals linked to the MGNREGA MIS.
  - **MGNREGA Ombudsperson:** An independent authority appointed at the district level in many states specifically to receive, investigate, and resolve MGNREGA-related complaints, including those involving delayed payments, denial of work, and financial irregularities.
- Because MGNREGA employment is a **legal entitlement**, denial of work or unresolved delays can, in principle, also be challenged through administrative appeal mechanisms and, ultimately, the courts.

## 17. Complete End-to-End Process Flow

The full lifecycle of a MGNREGA engagement, from a household's first contact with the scheme to receiving wages, typically follows these steps:

1. **Registration:** An adult member of a rural household applies to the Gram Panchayat for registration, submitting a simple application with a photograph.
2. **Verification:** The Gram Panchayat verifies residence and household composition locally.
3. **Job card issuance:** A free job card is issued to the household within 15 days, listing all adult members willing to work.
4. **Application for work:** Using the job card, a household member submits a written or oral application specifying the period of work needed, and receives a dated receipt.
5. **Work allocation:** The Gram Panchayat/Programme Officer allocates work from the pre-approved shelf of projects (recommended by the Gram Sabha) within 15 days of the application — or, failing that, the unemployment allowance becomes payable.
6. **Execution of work:** Workers perform unskilled manual labour at the worksite; attendance and progress are logged on muster rolls; mandated worksite facilities (water, shade, first aid, crèche) must be available.
7. **Measurement and wage computation:** Technical staff measure completed work against the Schedule of Rates to compute wages due.
8. **Wage payment:** Wages are credited directly to the worker's bank/post office account within 15 days of muster roll closure, via Direct Benefit Transfer; delayed payment triggers compensation.
9. **Social audit:** Every six months, the Gram Sabha publicly reviews the works, expenditure, and records associated with the scheme in its area.
10. **Grievance redressal (if needed):** Any complaints are escalated through the Programme Officer, District Programme Coordinator, state helpline/portal, or Ombudsperson.
11. **Asset completion and record-keeping:** Completed works (roads, ponds, irrigation structures, etc.) become part of the village's durable public assets, and are recorded in the MGNREGA MIS along with employment and expenditure data for that project.

## 18. Where to Find Authoritative, Current Information

- The **MGNREGA Management Information System (MIS)**, maintained by the Ministry of Rural Development, is the authoritative online source for current wage rates, employment and expenditure statistics, works data, and scheme notifications at the state, district, and panchayat level.
- Because **wage rates, budget allocations, and specific numerical figures change every financial year** (wage revisions typically take effect from 1 April), any time-sensitive figure should be verified against the latest official notification rather than treated as fixed.
