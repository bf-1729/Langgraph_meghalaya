# PMAY-Gramin (Pradhan Mantri Awas Yojana - Gramin) — Complete Scheme Reference

## 1. Overview

Pradhan Mantri Awas Yojana - Gramin (PMAY-G) is the rural component of the Government of India's flagship affordable housing mission, launched to achieve **"Housing for All"** by providing financial assistance and technical support to help eligible rural households construct or improve a permanent (pucca) home.

PMAY-G is implemented by the **Ministry of Rural Development (MoRD)**, in partnership with state governments and Gram Panchayats.

### Legal Status: Scheme vs. Statutory Right
PMAY-G is a **government scheme/mission implemented through executive guidelines and budgetary allocation**, not a law passed by Parliament. This is an important distinction from a rights-based law: there is **no individual legal entitlement to a house** under PMAY-G. Sanction of a house depends on a beneficiary being identified/approved through the scheme's eligibility and selection process (SECC/Awaas+ list and Gram Sabha approval) and on availability of budgetary allocation, not on a court-enforceable guarantee. This differs fundamentally from statutory entitlement schemes.

### What is a "Pucca House"?
A **pucca house** is a permanent, all-weather dwelling constructed with durable materials (such as burnt brick, stone, cement, or concrete walls and roof) that can withstand normal wear, weather, and natural elements over a long period. It is contrasted with a **kutcha house**, built from temporary or non-durable materials (such as mud, thatch, unburnt brick, or bamboo), which requires frequent repair and offers little protection from weather. Under PMAY-G, "not owning a pucca house anywhere in India" is a core eligibility condition, since the scheme is targeted at households that currently lack adequate permanent housing; a household living in a kutcha or severely damaged house remains eligible even if they technically "own" that structure.

## 2. Launch and Administrative Timeline

- **PMAY-Gramin (PMAY-G):** Launched with effect from **1 April 2016**, by restructuring the earlier **Indira Awaas Yojana (IAY)** into a new, more transparent, data-driven framework, implemented by the Ministry of Rural Development. The Union Cabinet has since approved continuation of PMAY-G for the period **FY 2024-25 to FY 2028-29**, targeting an additional 2 crore houses (cumulative target of around 4.95 crore houses) to cover the Awaas+ (2018) list and remaining eligible households from the SECC 2011 Permanent Wait List.
- PMAY was launched under the leadership of Prime Minister Narendra Modi, in pursuance of the government's stated vision of "Housing for All."

## 3. Objectives of the Scheme

1. **"Housing for All":** The central objective is to ensure every eligible rural family has access to a permanent, pucca house with basic amenities.
2. **Affordable home ownership:** Making home ownership accessible to eligible rural households through direct financial assistance for construction.
3. **Basic amenities and dignified living:** Ensuring PMAY-G houses are converged with access to toilets, drinking water, electricity, and clean cooking fuel, not just four walls and a roof.
4. **Women's empowerment and joint ownership:** Promoting house allotment in the name of the female head of household, or joint ownership between spouses, to strengthen women's property rights.
5. **Social equity:** Prioritising SC/ST households, minorities, persons with disabilities, widows, single women, manual scavengers, and other vulnerable/underprivileged groups.
6. **Technology-enabled, transparent delivery:** Using digital tools — geo-tagging, Aadhaar-based verification, real-time MIS dashboards — to ensure funds reach genuine beneficiaries and construction progress is independently verifiable.

## 4. PMAY-Gramin (PMAY-G): Structure

- PMAY-G's objective is to provide a pucca house with basic amenities to rural households that are **houseless** or living in **kutcha/severely damaged houses**.
- **Unit assistance:** ₹1.20 lakh per house in plain areas, and ₹1.30 lakh per house in North-Eastern and Himalayan states/hilly/difficult areas and Integrated Action Plan (IAP) districts.
- **Minimum house size:** 25 sqm, including a dedicated area for hygienic cooking (enhanced from the earlier Indira Awaas Yojana norm of 20 sqm).
- **Optional top-up loan:** Beneficiaries who choose to can avail an additional loan (facilitated up to around ₹70,000) from financial institutions to build a larger or better-finished house.
- **Toilet integration:** Construction of a toilet is a **mandatory, integral part** of the PMAY-G house, funded through convergence with the Swachh Bharat Mission-Gramin (SBM-G) or MGNREGA; a house is treated as "complete" only once its toilet is also constructed.
- **Construction timeline:** The house is expected to be completed within **12 months** of the date of sanction.
- **National Technical Support Agency (NTSA):** Envisaged at the national level to improve the quality of construction under PMAY-G.

## 5. Eligibility Criteria

### 5.1 General/Common Conditions
- The applicant's household must **not own a pucca (permanent) house** anywhere in India, in the name of any family member.
- The household must **not have previously availed central assistance** under any housing scheme from the Government of India or a state government, and must not have already received a PMAY subsidy.

### 5.2 PMAY-Gramin Beneficiary Identification
- Beneficiaries are identified using **Socio-Economic Caste Census (SECC), 2011** data, supplemented by the subsequent **Awaas+ (2018) survey**, rather than a simple BPL/income test.
- The eligible universe includes households that are **houseless**, or living in **zero, one, or two-room kutcha houses** with kutcha walls and kutcha roof.
- Priority is assigned using objectively verifiable deprivation parameters (housing condition, social category, vulnerability indicators), not purely income.
- Selection follows a **three-step verification process**: (i) SECC/Awaas+ data-based shortlisting, (ii) validation and approval by the **Gram Sabha**, and (iii) **geo-tagging** of the house/location to confirm ground reality before sanction.
- **Objections/appeals on the beneficiary list:** The draft priority list is placed before the Gram Sabha, where any household believing it has been wrongly excluded, or any member objecting to another household's inclusion, can raise the issue for local verification and correction before the list is finalised. A household **left out of the original SECC 2011 data** can be newly covered through the **Awaas+ survey** — self-registering via the AwaasPlus app (with Aadhaar-based identity verification) so it can be considered in the next selection cycle, rather than needing to wait for another full census.
- If a household believes it has been wrongly excluded even after Gram Sabha review, it can escalate through the block/district rural development office, or file a formal grievance (see Section 10).

## 6. Application and Registration Process

- PMAY-G does not rely on individual applications in the same way as many other schemes; rather, eligible households are **identified from the SECC/Awaas+ list**, verified locally, and approved by the Gram Sabha.
- At registration on the **AwaasSoft** system, the beneficiary's bank account details, nominee name, Aadhaar number, and MGNREGA job card number (where applicable) are captured.
- A geo-tagged photograph of the beneficiary in front of their current dwelling and the proposed construction site is captured using the **AwaasApp** and uploaded to AwaasSoft as part of registration.

## 7. Funding Pattern (Centre–State Cost Sharing)

- **Plain areas:** Cost shared between Centre and State in a **60:40 ratio**.
- **North-Eastern and Himalayan states** (and certain hill/difficult-area UTs): **90:10 ratio** (Centre:State).
- **Union Territories without a legislature:** **100% central funding**.
- Of the annual budgetary grant for PMAY-G, **95% is earmarked for house construction** (including a smaller allocation for administrative expenses within this), and **5% is retained at the central level as a reserve fund for special projects**.
- The annual financial allocation to states is based on the **Annual Action Plan (AAP)** approved by the **Empowered Committee**.

## 8. Governance and Institutional Structure

- **Ministry of Rural Development (MoRD):** The nodal central ministry.
- **Empowered Committee:** Approves the state-wise Annual Action Plan (AAP) that determines fund allocation.
- **State Rural Development Department:** Coordinates state-level implementation.
- **Gram Panchayat / Gram Sabha:** Validates and approves the local beneficiary list drawn from SECC/Awaas+ data, and oversees ground-level implementation.
- **National Technical Support Agency (NTSA):** Provides technical support to improve construction quality nationally.

## 9. Disbursement Mechanism: Installments and Verification

- Financial assistance under PMAY-G construction is released to beneficiaries in **staged installments linked to construction progress**, rather than as a single lump sum, to ensure funds are used for actual construction.
- **Direct Benefit Transfer (DBT):** Central assistance (and matching state share) is credited **directly into the beneficiary's bank account**, based on the verified/geo-tagged construction stage, transferred through PFMS.
- **AwaasSoft/AwaasApp:** The system used to register beneficiaries, record geo-tagged photographs of the house at each stage, and trigger installment releases as construction progresses.
- The final installment is typically withheld until the **toilet has also been constructed**.
- **Construction completion timeline:** PMAY-G houses are expected to be completed within **12 months** of sanction (see Section 4).

## 10. Monitoring, Digital Systems, and Transparency Mechanisms

- **AwaasSoft:** The centralised MIS for rural housing, recording beneficiary details, fund flow, and construction status at the household level.
- **Third-party quality monitoring and social audits:** PMAY-G provides for independent, third-party monitoring of construction quality, alongside community-level social audits, with defined financial norms for states to conduct these activities.
- **Public disclosure of geo-tagged images:** Geo-tagged house images, as moderated by states/UTs, are made available in the public domain for transparency.
- **Cross-linkage with PMAY-Urban MIS:** Used specifically to check for and prevent duplicate beneficiaries across the urban and rural schemes.

## 11. Convergence with Other Government Schemes

PMAY-G is deliberately converged with several other central schemes to deliver a complete, liveable home rather than a bare structure:
- **MGNREGA:** Provides **90–95 person-days of unskilled wage employment** to a PMAY-G beneficiary for the construction of their own house, over and above the housing unit assistance, effectively letting the beneficiary "earn" part of their own labour contribution.
- **Swachh Bharat Mission-Gramin (SBM-G):** Funds the **mandatory toilet** (around ₹12,000) built as part of every PMAY-G house; a house is not treated as complete without it.
- **Saubhagya / rural electrification schemes:** For electricity connections to PMAY-G houses.
- **Ujjwala Yojana:** For clean cooking fuel (LPG) connections.
- **Drinking water and livelihood support programmes:** For piped water access and, in some cases, linked livelihood assistance for the beneficiary household.
- This convergence model means a single PMAY-G beneficiary may draw support from several schemes simultaneously (housing assistance, MGNREGA wage-days, SBM-G toilet funding, Saubhagya electricity, Ujjwala LPG) to reach a fully liveable home with basic amenities.

## 12. Grievance Redressal

- Grievances (e.g., beneficiary list disputes, delayed Fund Transfer Orders (FTOs), missing geo-tag uploads, incorrect installment amounts) can be raised with the **Gram Panchayat** or the **Block Development Officer (BDO)** in the first instance.
- If unresolved at the local level, complaints can be filed online through **CPGRAMS (pgportal.gov.in)**, selecting the Ministry of Rural Development and the PMAY-G scheme.
- Common troubleshooting steps used by officials include checking the beneficiary's status and Fund Transfer Order (FTO) date on AwaasSoft, verifying whether required stage photographs/geo-tags have been uploaded, and confirming the beneficiary's Aadhaar-bank seeding status if a processed payment has not reached the account.
- Since PMAY-G is a scheme rather than a statutory entitlement (see Section 1), grievance redressal here functions as an **administrative correction mechanism** rather than a legally enforceable right to compensation, unlike some provisions under statutory schemes.

## 13. Penalties, Misuse, and Corrective Provisions

- **Fund misutilisation by beneficiaries:** If sanctioned housing assistance is found to be misused (e.g., diverted for a purpose other than house construction, or construction is not completed), implementing authorities can withhold subsequent installments, and in confirmed misuse cases, initiate recovery of disbursed funds through applicable state revenue recovery procedures.
- **Action against fraudulent/duplicate beneficiaries:** Cross-verification between the PMAY-U and PMAY-G MIS systems, and Aadhaar-based de-duplication (via UIDAI integration), is specifically used to detect and remove duplicate or ineligible beneficiaries from PMAY-G.
- **Utilisation Certificates (UCs):** States and implementing agencies are required to submit Utilisation Certificates confirming that previously released installments were spent on the sanctioned purpose, as a precondition for release of the next installment of central assistance — a routine financial-compliance safeguard used throughout PMAY-G fund flows.
- **RTI-based public accountability:** Beneficiary-list and subsidy data are considered public information; officials/citizens can seek specific verification records (e.g., an individual household's eligibility verification report) through a formal **Right to Information (RTI)** request where MIS data alone doesn't resolve a dispute.

## 14. Special Provisions for Vulnerable Groups

- **Women:** PMAY-G promotes house allotment in the **name of the female head of household**, or **joint ownership** between spouses, to strengthen women's property rights; special targets/reporting exist for houses sanctioned to women, including single women and widows.
- **SC/ST households, minorities, and manual scavengers:** Given explicit priority in beneficiary identification and selection under PMAY-G guidelines.
- **Persons with disabilities:** Considered a priority category for allotment and assistance under the scheme's vulnerability parameters.

## 15. Checking Application/Beneficiary Status

- Beneficiaries and officials can check status through the **AwaasSoft portal/AwaasApp** or by contacting the **Gram Panchayat**, using the registered beneficiary ID.

## 16. Complete End-to-End Process Flow

The lifecycle of a PMAY-G house, from identification to handover, broadly follows these stages:

1. **Identification:** Eligible households are identified from the SECC 2011/Awaas+ deprivation-based list.
2. **Verification:** The **Gram Panchayat/Gram Sabha** verifies eligibility and non-ownership of a pucca house.
3. **Registration on MIS:** Beneficiary details, bank account, Aadhaar, and MGNREGA job card number are captured on **AwaasSoft**.
4. **Approval and sanction:** The **Gram Sabha** approves the beneficiary list.
5. **Baseline geo-tagging:** A geo-tagged photograph of the beneficiary's current dwelling and proposed construction site is captured (via **AwaasApp**) before construction begins.
6. **Staged fund release:** Financial assistance is released in installments, each triggered by verified, geo-tagged confirmation of the corresponding construction stage — foundation, roof-level/lintel, and so on — rather than as a lump sum.
7. **Construction and monitoring:** Progress is tracked through the multi-stage geo-tagging system, cross-checked by third-party quality monitors and, where applicable, social audits.
8. **Convergence with other schemes:** The mandatory toilet is completed (SBM-G/MGNREGA funding), and, where applicable, electricity (Saubhagya), LPG (Ujjwala), and water connections are arranged.
9. **Completion verification:** Once the final construction stage — including the toilet — is geo-tagged and verified as complete, the final installment is released.
10. **Handover and registration as completed:** The house is recorded as completed in the scheme's MIS, generally in the name of the female head of household or as joint ownership, and the beneficiary formally takes possession.
11. **Grievance redressal (if needed at any stage):** Disputes over eligibility, delayed installments, or construction issues are escalated through the Gram Panchayat/BDO, and if unresolved, through CPGRAMS (see Section 12).

## 17. Where to Find Authoritative, Current Information

- The official **PMAY-Gramin portal** (pmayg.nic.in), maintained by the Ministry of Rural Development, is the authoritative source for current eligibility criteria, unit assistance amounts, guidelines, and application/beneficiary status.
- Because **unit costs, subsidy amounts, and mission timelines are periodically revised** by government notification, any time-sensitive figure should be verified against the latest official guideline rather than treated as permanently fixed.
