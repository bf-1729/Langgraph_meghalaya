# CM-ELEVATE — General FAQ

### What is CM-ELEVATE?
CM-ELEVATE is the **Chief Minister of Meghalaya's flagship credit-linked entrepreneurship programme**, launched by Chief Minister Conrad K. Sangma. It offers a capital subsidy of **35% to 75% of project cost**, combined with bank credit linkage and handholding support, to individuals and entities starting a business across 15 covered sectors — from livestock farming to tourism to entertainment venues.

### Is CM-ELEVATE a central (Government of India) scheme like MGNREGA?
No. CM-ELEVATE is a **Meghalaya state government scheme**, run through executive decision and budgetary allocation, not a Government of India programme and not a law of the Meghalaya Legislative Assembly. There is no individual legal entitlement to the subsidy — it depends on application, eligibility screening, selection under the relevant sub-scheme, and fund availability.

### When was CM-ELEVATE launched?
**13 October 2023**, at an integrated launch event in Tura, positioned as the successor to the earlier PRIME programme.

### Who is eligible for CM-ELEVATE?
Individuals resident in Meghalaya, registered business entities, and non-registered collectives such as Self-Help Groups (SHGs), Village Organizations (VOs) and Producer Groups (PGs) — provided the proposed venture fits one of the 15 covered sectors, or the flexible "Any Business Venture" category if it does not.

### How does someone apply for CM-ELEVATE?
Through the single-window **MeghalayaOne portal** (meghalayaone.gov.in), selecting the relevant sub-scheme for their sector. PRIME Meghalaya facilitates screening, bank credit linkage and handholding/capacity-building support alongside the application.

### How many individual schemes does CM-ELEVATE cover?
Fifteen. Publicly announced sub-schemes include Piggery, Poultry, Goat Farming, Dairy Development, Warehouse, PRIME Tourism Vehicle, PRIME Agriculture Response Vehicle, Chief Ministers Green Taxi, Motorcaravan, Sericulture and Weaving, Agro Tourism Villa, PRIME Small Enterprise Empowerment, Cinema Theatre, Sports and Wellness Centre, and Any Business Venture.

### What is a Producer Group, SHG or VO in this context?
The same collective-applicant categories used across Meghalaya's rural livelihood schemes: a Self-Help Group (SHG) is a village-level savings/credit collective; a Village Organization (VO) federates several SHGs; a Producer Group (PG) is a collective sharing one income-generating activity. Any of the three may apply to CM-ELEVATE as a "registered" or "unregistered" entity depending on its own registration status.

### How much financial support does CM-ELEVATE provide?
Publicly, a capital subsidy of 35% to 75% of total project cost, varying by sector — for example 50% financial aid is specifically cited for the Agriculture Department's polyhouse component. This is policy-level information; the curated application data this assistant queries does not hold a sanctioned-amount, subsidy, loan or disbursement figure for any individual application — see the note below.

### Who implements CM-ELEVATE?
**PRIME (Promotion and Retention of Investment and Meghalaya's Entrepreneurs)**, the state's entrepreneurship ecosystem body, runs the programme's public-facing page, training centres and portal presence, in coordination with the relevant line department for each sector (e.g. Tourism Department for vehicle/homestay/villa schemes, Agriculture Department for the polyhouse component).

### What is "PRIME" and how does it relate to CM-ELEVATE?
PRIME is the predecessor programme CM-ELEVATE is explicitly framed as building on and extending — and three of CM-ELEVATE's 15 curated sub-schemes carry the "PRIME" name directly (PRIME Small Enterprise Empowerment and Development (SEED), PRIME Tourism Vehicle Scheme, PRIME Agriculture Response Vehicle Scheme).

### What reported figures exist for CM-ELEVATE?
Point-in-time figures from Chief Minister's Office press releases and PRIME Meghalaya's website: an entrepreneur-reach target of 20,000+ individuals; an investment scale of roughly ₹300 crore over three years as stated at the October 2023 launch; and an anticipated ~700 direct jobs. Because the programme has continued to add and notify sub-schemes through at least December 2025, cumulative and target figures should be treated as point-in-time, not final.

### Is "Common Facility Centre (CFC)" a CM-ELEVATE scheme?
It has been publicly announced as one (notified 11 March 2024). It does **not**, however, appear among the 15 schemes in the curated application database this assistant queries — a data question asking for CFC application counts will come back as "not found in this data", not zero, because the scheme itself is outside the loaded dataset rather than merely empty.

### How is CM-ELEVATE different from MGNREGA, PMAY-G and Focus Plus in this assistant's data?
CM-ELEVATE is the only one of the four schemes with **no numeric measure and no date column at all** in the curated database — `COUNT(*)` (an application count) is the entire aggregate vocabulary, and no year, month or financial year can be attached to an application. Every application record belongs to exactly one of the 15 sub-schemes, so most figures are naturally reported per sub-scheme rather than as one programme-wide total. "On hold" always means the application's data-verification outcome, never a separate on-hold flag. None of the subsidy percentages or investment figures in this FAQ or the complete reference document are derivable from the application data — they are policy-level figures, not per-application amounts.

### Where can official, up-to-date CM-ELEVATE information be checked?
PRIME Meghalaya's official CM-ELEVATE page (primemeghalaya.com/cm-elevate) and Beneficiaries page; the MeghalayaOne portal (meghalayaone.gov.in) for applications; and Chief Minister's Office, Government of Meghalaya press releases (meghalaya.gov.in) for launch and notification announcements.
