# FOCUS (Farmers' Collectivization for Upscaling Production and Marketing Systems) — Complete Scheme Reference

## 1. Overview

FOCUS is a flagship farmer-welfare and agri-economic scheme of the **Government of Meghalaya**. Its name stands for **"Farmers' Collectivization for Upscaling Production and Marketing Systems."** The scheme works by organising farmers into **Producer Groups (PGs)** — farmer collectives — and then channelling direct financial assistance and value-chain support to members of these groups, with the aim of upscaling farming activity, strengthening market linkages, and raising farmer incomes.

The scheme is described by the state government as an **innovative, inclusive welfare programme**, conceived partly in response to the economic hardship faced by Meghalaya's farming community during the COVID-19 pandemic. It is closely associated with Meghalaya Chief Minister **Conrad K. Sangma**, who has personally led most major FOCUS cheque-distribution and disbursal events across the state.

FOCUS is the **foundational scheme** in a family of related state programmes, including sector-specific Mission Mode projects that FOCUS helps fund.

## 2. Naming and Launch Timeline

- **2018:** The Government of Meghalaya first launched the **Lakadong Mission**, an earlier, narrower initiative to scale up Lakadong turmeric production — part of the same lineage of state agri-value-chain thinking that FOCUS later expanded on.
- **2020–21 (exact month varies by source):** FOCUS was launched during the COVID-19 period as a response to the economic hardship faced by farmers. Official and news sources give slightly different launch months — some cite **March 2021**, while a February 2022 report quotes the Chief Minister describing FOCUS as having been "launched in September last year" (i.e., **September 2021**). Both figures appear in credible reporting; the exact month should be verified against an official state government notification if precision is required.
- **2021 (through the year):** FOCUS rollout proceeded block-by-block across the state, with cheque-distribution events held in individual C&RD Blocks (e.g., Samanda block, East Garo Hills, in September 2021; Salmanpara, South West Garo Hills, in February 2021 reporting).
- **February 2022:** Chief Minister Conrad K. Sangma stated that FOCUS, with an estimated budget of **₹200 crore**, was targeting over **4.5 lakh farmers**, each receiving ₹5,000 in financial assistance.
- **October 2022:** FOCUS's benefit was formally extended to **urban Producer Groups** (non-farm income-generating collectives of 10 or more members).
- **2023–2025:** Disbursal to Producer Groups has continued in periodic tranches; by mid-2025, cumulative disbursal under FOCUS was reported at around **₹120 crore to over 22,000 Producer Groups**, benefiting roughly **2.1 lakh rural households** across the state.
- **June 2025:** A tranche of nearly **₹19 crore** was disbursed statewide to Producer Groups under FOCUS (₹8 crore of it to over 4,600 PGs in Garo Hills alone), described as part of the FY 2025–26 disbursal round.

## 3. Objectives of the Scheme

1. **Farmer welfare and income support:** Providing direct financial assistance to farmers to cushion economic hardship and support their livelihoods, originally conceived against the backdrop of COVID-19-related distress.
2. **Collectivisation of farmers:** Organising farmers into **Producer Groups** so that they can access support, inputs, and markets collectively rather than individually, improving bargaining power and economies of scale.
3. **Upscaling production:** Providing seed/mobilisation capital that Producer Group members can use to expand and improve their existing farming or income-generating activities.
4. **Strengthening the marketing system:** Supporting value-chain development, machinery, transportation, and aggregation of produce so that farm output can reach markets more efficiently and profitably — i.e., "farm to market" interventions, not just production support.
5. **Doubling farmer incomes:** Positioned by the state government as a central pillar of its broader goal of doubling the income of Meghalaya's farmers.
6. **Supporting Mission Mode commodity sectors:** Channelling farmers toward focused, high-value agri sub-sectors identified by the state, including **Lakadong turmeric, piggery, milk/dairy, spices, ginger, and aroma (essential-oil) crops**.
7. **Extending inclusion to non-farm livelihoods:** Later widened to bring urban, non-farm income-generating collectives (e.g., weaving groups, food-processing groups, youth enterprise groups) into the same Producer-Group-based support model.
8. **Encouraging self-sustaining group finance:** The Chief Minister has specifically encouraged Producer Groups to use disbursed funds partly as **"revolving funds"** among members, so that the benefit compounds and recirculates within the group rather than being a one-off payout.

## 4. Key Structural Features

- **Grant, not a loan:** Government representatives have repeatedly and explicitly clarified that the financial assistance under FOCUS is an outright **assistance/grant**, not a loan requiring repayment.
- **Collectivisation model — the Producer Group (PG):** The scheme's core organising unit. Farmers (or other income-generating individuals, once the scheme was extended to urban groups) come together to form a PG in order to access the benefit, even though the cash itself is credited to individual members.
- **Minimum group size (urban PGs):** Urban Producer Groups require a minimum of **10 members** engaged in a shared income-generating activity to register as a group. (No separate minimum size figure is consistently published for rural/farming PGs in available official material; group sizes reported in disbursal events have varied widely, e.g., "170 Producer Groups" or "42 Producer Groups" receiving cheques in a single round.)
- **Direct financial assistance per member:** An **initial mobilisation fund of ₹5,000 per Producer Group member**, intended as seed money for upscaling farming or income-generating activity.
- **Cheque/DBT-based disbursal:** Disbursal has been carried out both via **cheques handed over at public distribution events** (particularly in the scheme's earlier years) and via **direct transfer to bank accounts**, especially as the scheme matured and digital payment tracking (e.g., through the MeghalayaOne portal) became more central.
- **Value-chain support beyond cash:** Beyond the initial cash mobilisation fund, FOCUS is described as providing support in **funding, value-chain development, machinery support, market linkages, transportation, and aggregation of produce** — i.e., ongoing production and marketing support, not just a one-time payment.
- **Block-level rollout:** Implementation and cheque-distribution events are organised through Meghalaya's **Community & Rural Development (C&RD) Blocks** (56 blocks statewide as of late-2025 reporting; earlier reporting cited 52).
- **Linked Mission Mode investment:** The government has cited a much larger pool of funding (e.g., **₹700 crore** referenced in a 2021 speech) allocated under associated **Mission Mode programmes** to create livelihood opportunities in sectors like Lakadong turmeric, piggery, and spices — complementing the direct PG cash assistance under FOCUS itself.

## 5. Eligibility Criteria

Based on official government announcements and reporting:

- **Farmers organised into (or willing to organise into) a Producer Group** are the primary eligible unit — the individual benefit (₹5,000) accrues to each PG member.
- **Rural farming households** across Meghalaya were the scheme's original target base, with a stated ambition of reaching over **4.5 lakh farmers** statewide.
- **Urban Producer Groups** of **10 or more members** engaged in a shared income-generating activity (weaving, pickle-making, other small-enterprise or youth-led activities) became eligible from **October 2022** onward, extending the scheme beyond agriculture.
- The registration window for a given round is announced periodically (e.g., an October–November 2022 window was open till the end of November), with fund release following in a subsequent tranche.
- There is **no single, centrally published statutory eligibility list** (unlike a central Act-based scheme such as MGNREGA); exact eligibility, group-formation requirements, and documentation for a given registration/disbursal round are notified locally through the **C&RD Block offices** and the **Department of Agriculture and Farmers' Empowerment** at the time of each round, so current applicants should confirm details at the nearest C&RD Block office or via the MeghalayaOne portal.

## 6. Registration and Disbursal Process

1. **Formation/identification of a Producer Group:** Farmers (or, after the 2022 expansion, urban income-generating groups) organise into a PG of the required composition.
2. **Registration at the block level:** Groups register with the local C&RD Block office as part of the scheme's rollout in that block.
3. **Verification:** Local officials verify the group's composition and activity.
4. **Cheque distribution / fund release event:** Historically, funds have often been released at public events where the Chief Minister or other officials personally distribute cheques to a batch of Producer Groups from a district or block (e.g., "30 Producer Groups… 10 from each block" at one event; "42 producer groups" at another; "170 Producer Groups" at another).
5. **Credit to members:** Once released, the ₹5,000 mobilisation fund is credited to individual members of each PG.
6. **Use of funds:** Groups are encouraged to use the funds as working/seed capital for their farming or income-generating activity, and — per the Chief Minister's guidance — to circulate a portion as a **revolving fund** within the group.

## 6a. Documents Typically Required

There is no single, centrally published official checklist of documents for FOCUS (the scheme runs on periodic administrative notifications rather than one fixed statutory form), but based on how the scheme and comparable Meghalaya DBT schemes are administered, applicants/Producer Group members are generally expected to have:

- **Proof of identity** — Aadhaar Card, Voter ID, or another valid government-issued photo ID.
- **Meghalaya Family ID / Citizen ID** — increasingly used across Meghalaya government schemes to verify residency and household identity; some scheme listings specifically require each PG member to hold a valid Family ID.
- **Proof of residence** in the relevant village/block (for rural PGs) or locality (for urban PGs).
- **Bank account details** — a bank passbook or account statement in the applicant's own name, since the benefit is paid via Direct Benefit Transfer (DBT) or cheque.
- **Producer Group formation/registration record** — documentation showing the group's members, the shared farming or income-generating activity they are engaged in, and (for urban PGs) confirmation that the group has at least 10 members.
- **Passport-size photograph**, where required for the FOCUS+/FOCUS card or local registration form.
- Any **additional form or Annexure** prescribed by the C&RD Block office at the time of a given registration round.

Because exact documentation requirements are set locally by the C&RD Block/Department of Agriculture and Farmers' Empowerment for each registration round, applicants should confirm the current list with their local C&RD Block office or the MeghalayaOne portal before applying.

## 7. Financial Assistance Structure

- **Per-member benefit:** ₹5,000 initial mobilisation fund per Producer Group member.
- **Scheme-level budget (as stated in Feb 2022):** An estimated **₹200 crore** budget, targeting more than **4.5 lakh farmers**.
- **Cumulative disbursal reported (by mid-2025):** Close to **₹120 crore disbursed to over 22,000 Producer Groups**, benefiting around **2.1 lakh rural households** — with roughly ₹8 crore/4,600+ PGs in Garo Hills and ₹19 crore statewide in one recent (FY 2025–26) tranche alone.
- **Associated Mission Mode funding:** A separate, larger pool (₹700 crore cited in 2021) has been allocated to Mission Mode livelihood projects in sectors like Lakadong turmeric, piggery, and spices, which work alongside — but are distinct from — the direct per-member FOCUS cash assistance.
- Because **budget figures, cumulative disbursal totals, and beneficiary counts are updated with each new tranche and financial year**, current figures should be verified against the latest official government announcement rather than treated as fixed.

## 8. Focus Sectors / Mission Mode Projects

FOCUS funding and associated Mission Mode investment are concentrated on a set of state-identified high-value agricultural sub-sectors, described as receiving support "from farm to market":

- **Lakadong turmeric** — a GI-tagged, high-curcumin (7–12%) turmeric variety grown mainly in the Jaintia Hills, and a flagship product for the state (further scaled up from 2026 through the much larger, separate **Mission Golden Spice** project).
- **Piggery**
- **Milk/dairy**
- **Spices** (more broadly, beyond turmeric)
- **Ginger**
- **Aroma** (essential-oil/aromatic crops)
- **Poultry and horticulture** (mentioned in connection with productivity-linked PG support)

These Mission Mode projects are supported through collective marketing centres, integrated village cooperative societies, farmers' markets, and processing centres, and are reported to benefit well over a lakh households across the state.

## 9. Institutional Structure

- **Government of Meghalaya, Office of the Chief Minister:** Drives the scheme's flagship launches and major cheque-distribution/disbursal events; Chief Minister Conrad K. Sangma has personally led the large majority of publicised FOCUS events.
- **Department of Agriculture and Farmers' Empowerment / Ministry of Agriculture and Farmers' Welfare, Meghalaya:** The nodal department for the scheme's agricultural components and Mission Mode projects (Agriculture & Farmers' Welfare Minister as of recent reporting: **Timothy D. Shira**).
- **Community & Rural Development (C&RD) Department / C&RD Blocks:** The block-level administrative units (56 statewide as of late-2025 reporting) through which registration, verification, and local rollout of FOCUS are coordinated.
- **Producer Groups (PGs):** The grassroots collective unit — rural and, from October 2022, urban — through which members access and often pool/revolve scheme funds.
- **MeghalayaOne portal (meghalayaone.gov.in):** The state's digital citizen-services portal, increasingly used for registration and status tracking for FOCUS and related schemes.

## 10. Where to Find Authoritative, Current Information

- The **Government of Meghalaya's official press release channel** (Office of the Chief Minister, Media & Communications Cell) publishes scheme launch and disbursal announcements.
- The **MyMeg portal** (mymeg.meghalaya.gov.in) lists FOCUS scheme details by sector, district, and block.
- The **MeghalayaOne portal** (meghalayaone.gov.in) is the state's citizen-facing digital portal for scheme registration and status-checking.
- The **Department of Agriculture and Farmers' Empowerment / C&RD Department** websites publish scheme notifications and updated figures.
- Because **launch dates cited in different sources vary slightly (March vs. September 2021), and budget, disbursal, and beneficiary figures are updated with each new tranche**, any time-sensitive or precise figure should be verified against the latest official government notification rather than treated as fixed.
